Goal Hierarchy Note

Final success should be defined by an externally meaningful threshold rather than by internal model metrics alone. In the present context, the clearest ultimate goal is a reading sufficient to satisfy the current contest threshold, presently understood as at least 10 legible letters or an equivalent recognized result.

This suggests a hierarchy of goals:

1. Final success
   A result sufficient to satisfy the current contest threshold, presently understood as at least 10 legible letters or an equivalent recognized reading.

2. Strong intermediate success
   Repeated, defensible partial legibility on real scroll material that appears stable, reproducible, and not artifact-driven.

3. Weak intermediate success
   Improved coherence, stability, or plausibility that may justify further testing, but does not yet amount to reading.

This hierarchy is useful because it keeps the project from confusing lower loss, cleaner-looking outputs, or more visually appealing artifacts with actual success. It also provides a more disciplined way to discuss progress below the prize threshold without pretending that such progress is easy to capture in a single scalar metric.
